# Fagamusta
Famagusta Historical Sites
1. St. Simeon Church
St. Simeon is a small medieval Orthodox church in the south-eastern part of the walled city of Famagusta. It was associated with a monastery connected with Saint Catherine's Monastery at Sinai. It is one of the surviving Byzantine-era churches of Famagusta and is associated with the city's early Orthodox history.
2. Lala Mustafa Pasha Mosque
Originally built as the Cathedral of Saint Nicholas, this Gothic cathedral was begun in 1298 and consecrated in 1328. It became an important medieval cathedral where the kings of Cyprus were crowned as kings of Jerusalem. After the Ottoman conquest of Famagusta in 1571, it was converted into a mosque. Its Gothic architecture remains one of the most important landmarks of the walled city.
3. Namik Kemal Dungeon and Museum
This two-storey Ottoman-period building stands on the site of the former Venetian Palace. The writer Namik Kemal was exiled to Cyprus in 1873 and imprisoned in Famagusta. He first stayed in the lower cell and was later moved to an upper room. He was pardoned in 1876. The building was restored and opened as a museum in 1993 and later reorganized as a modern museum.
4. Yirmisekiz Celebi Mehmet Efendi Tomb
Yirmisekiz Celebi Mehmet Efendi was an Ottoman statesman and diplomat. In 1720 he was sent to France as an Ottoman ambassador and later became known for his account of the mission. After the Patrona Halil uprising, he was exiled to Cyprus and died in Famagusta in 1732. His tomb is beside the former Church of SS Peter and Paul, also known as Bugday Mosque.
5. Venetian Palace Ruins
The Venetian Palace, also known as the Palazzo del Provveditore, was originally a royal palace associated with the Lusignan kings of Cyprus. The Venetians renovated it in the 16th century and introduced Renaissance features. Much of the palace was destroyed during the 1571 siege. The surviving arches, walls and courtyard are important remains of medieval and Venetian Famagusta.
6. St. George of the Latins Church
This Gothic church is generally dated to the late 13th or early 14th century and is one of the earliest Gothic monuments in Famagusta. It was a Latin church with a single nave, ribbed vaults and pointed arches. Much of the roof and parts of the walls were lost, with serious damage associated with the 1571 siege. The surviving ruins still show important Gothic architectural details.
